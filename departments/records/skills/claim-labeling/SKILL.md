# Claim Labeling

A page that states everything with equal confidence is lying about something.
Asgardr surfaces label every claim so the reader knows which kind of sentence
they are holding.

## The labels

- **CONFIRMED** — verified against a primary source; the source and its date are
  attached (see `source-dating`).
- **PRELIMINARY** — reported but not yet verified against a primary source, or
  verified against a secondary one. Says who reported it.
- **UNKNOWN** — no reliable value exists yet. **UNKNOWN is a publishable value.**
  Costs after a flood are carried as UNKNOWN until an official number exists —
  never estimated into false precision because a table cell looked empty.

For tracked work, the status vocabulary is fixed:
**proposed / awarded / under construction / complete / stalled** — and "stalled"
is used when true. A scorecard with no stalled entries after five years is a
brochure.

## Rules

- **The label sits with the claim**, in the sentence or the cell — not in a
  distant methodology note.
- **Vintage on every figure.** Population, scores, balances: state the release
  or measurement date next to the number. A number without a date drifts into a
  lie at an unknown rate.
- **Analysis is labeled as the notebook's own.** When facts are verified but the
  connection between them is the author's inference, the page says so — "the
  facts are verified; the connection is the notebook's own."
- **Downgrades are visible.** When CONFIRMED turns out wrong, the correction
  appends and the history stays readable (`discrepancy-ledger` owns the
  mechanics).

This applies to engineering claims exactly as to civic ones: "survives a node
loss" is CONFIRMED only with the drain artifact attached; otherwise it is
PRELIMINARY and says so.
